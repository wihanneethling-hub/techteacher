In my previous article, [*Your Child's Smartphone Isn't the Problem. Lack of Parental Involvement Is.*](/blog/child-smartphone-parental-involvement/), I briefly mentioned some of the tools parents can use to manage their children's devices. But choosing between those tools is not always straightforward.

Should you use the controls already built into an iPhone or Android device? Is Qustodio worth paying for? And what exactly does Bark do differently? The answer depends less on which app has the longest feature list and more on the problem you are trying to solve.

## Start With the Problem

Before installing anything, ask what you actually need the app to do. Are you trying to limit screen time, approve new apps, block inappropriate websites, track a younger child's location, manage several different device types, identify possible bullying or dangerous conversations, or monitor messages?

These are different problems, and no single tool handles all of them equally well. Apple Screen Time and Google Family Link are mainly designed to manage apps, content and device use. Qustodio offers more detailed controls across different platforms, while Bark focuses more heavily on detecting potentially concerning content and alerting parents when something may need attention.

## Apple Screen Time: Best for Apple Families

<div class="app-identity">
  <img src="/assets/app-logos/apple.svg" alt="Apple logo">
  <span>Screen Time is built into Apple devices</span>
</div>

For families using iPhones, iPads and Macs, [Apple Screen Time](https://support.apple.com/en-za/guide/iphone/iphbfa595995/ios) should usually be the starting point. Parents can view app and website activity, create downtime schedules, limit individual apps, restrict purchases and inappropriate content, and control who a child can communicate with during certain periods.

Apple also includes Communication Safety, which can detect potentially sensitive images and warn children before they view or send them. Screen Time is not a full monitoring platform. It will not analyse WhatsApp conversations or alert parents whenever a concerning topic appears, but many families do not need that level of oversight.

If the main concerns are excessive gaming, social media use, late-night access or inappropriate downloads, Screen Time may already be enough.

## Google Family Link: Best for Android

<div class="app-identity">
  <img src="/assets/app-logos/google-family-link.jpg" alt="Google Family Link app icon">
  <span>Google Family Link</span>
</div>

[Google Family Link](https://families.google/familylink/) plays a similar role for Android phones and Chromebooks. It allows parents to create daily limits, schedule downtime, block or approve apps, manage content settings and view a child's location. It also connects with Google services such as Play, Chrome, Search and YouTube.

Like Screen Time, Family Link is primarily a management tool. It helps parents create boundaries, but it does not provide detailed monitoring of private conversations across multiple social platforms. For most Android families, it is still the logical place to begin.

## Qustodio: Better for Mixed Devices

<div class="app-identity">
  <img src="/assets/app-logos/qustodio.jpg" alt="Qustodio app icon">
  <span>Qustodio Parental Control</span>
</div>

[Qustodio](https://www.qustodio.com/) becomes more appealing when a family uses a mixture of iPhones, Android phones, Windows computers and Macs. It combines web filtering, app blocking, schedules, location monitoring, activity reports and internet controls in one dashboard, while more advanced plans add broader monitoring features.

Its main strength is consistency across different platforms. Its main weakness is complexity. Some features work differently depending on the device, and deeper iPhone monitoring may require additional setup through a Mac or Windows computer.

Qustodio makes the most sense when the built-in Apple or Google tools are no longer enough and parents genuinely need more detailed cross-platform control.

## Bark: Designed to Find Warning Signs

<div class="app-identity">
  <img src="/assets/app-logos/bark.jpg" alt="Bark parental-controls app icon">
  <span>Bark Parental Controls</span>
</div>

[Bark](https://www.bark.us/) approaches parental control differently. Its main feature is not simply limiting apps. It analyses supported texts, emails, photos, videos and social media activity, then alerts parents when it detects possible concerns such as bullying, sexual content, self-harm or contact with online predators.

This can be less intrusive than reading every message manually because parents receive alerts when something may require attention rather than constantly checking everything their child says. Bark also includes screen-time schedules, filtering and location tools.

The challenge is that Bark is much easier to use fully on Android than on iPhone. Deeper iPhone monitoring generally requires a home computer, and some Bark hardware is not available in South Africa. Bark is most relevant when there is a genuine concern about harmful conversations or dangerous online behaviour. It is probably excessive if the goal is simply to stop TikTok working after 8 p.m.

## Microsoft Family Safety: Useful in the Right Home

<div class="app-identity">
  <img src="/assets/app-logos/microsoft-family-safety.jpg" alt="Microsoft Family Safety app icon">
  <span>Microsoft Family Safety</span>
</div>

[Microsoft Family Safety](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/family-safety) is worth considering when children spend significant time on Windows computers or Xbox consoles. It can manage device schedules, app and game limits, spending and activity reporting.

Its biggest limitation is that its strongest web filtering works through Microsoft Edge. That makes it less useful in households where children mainly use Chrome, Safari or mobile devices. It is a useful addition to a Microsoft-heavy household, but not the strongest all-round option for phone management.

## Which One Should You Choose?

My recommendation is fairly simple. Apple families should start with Screen Time, while Android or Chromebook families should begin with Google Family Link. Households using several different device types may benefit from Qustodio, and parents with serious concerns about bullying, self-harm, dangerous messages or online predators should investigate Bark. Microsoft Family Safety is most useful where Windows and Xbox play a major role.

The important point is not to install the most powerful monitoring product automatically. Start with the least intrusive tool that solves the actual problem.

A younger child receiving a first device may need strict app approval and time limits. An older teenager may need greater independence, more privacy and a different conversation about what monitoring is appropriate. The controls should change as the child grows.

## Protection Comes With a Privacy Trade-Off

There is a significant difference between limiting Instagram to 30 minutes and allowing a third-party service to analyse private messages, photographs and conversations. Sometimes deeper monitoring may be justified, but it should be a deliberate decision rather than simply another setting switched on during setup.

Children should generally know what is being monitored, what parents can see and why the controls are being introduced. Secret surveillance may reveal information, but it can also damage trust. The goal is not to catch children doing something wrong. It is to help them develop the judgement to make better decisions.

## The App Cannot Become the Parent

No parental-control app can understand the full context of a child's life. An alert may detect a concerning phrase, but it cannot always tell whether it was something serious, a joke or a friend asking for support. A screen-time report may show three hours on a device, but it cannot always distinguish between homework, creativity, communication and mindless scrolling.

The technology gives parents information. But parenting still requires us to interpret it, ask questions and have difficult conversations.

The best parental-control app is therefore not necessarily the one that monitors the most. It is the one that gives your family the right level of support without replacing trust, communication and active involvement. And in my opinion, above all, the decision about which app to use is less important than **the decision to start using one**.
